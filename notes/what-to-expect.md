# What to expect in the paper

This is inferred from the material you were given: 4 revision tests, 3 worksheets and the self-assessment. The actual paper may mix these differently, but these are the question types the course has been drilling.

## 1. Vocabulary MCQs (Sets A–D format)

50 questions, A–D. Counting all 200 question slots across the four sets, a 50-question paper works out to roughly:

| Type | Per paper | Looks like | How to attack |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Root → word** | ~8 | "Which word best demonstrates the root *mort*?" | Find the option that *contains* the root. The others use look-alike roots (fort, port) |
| **Root → meaning** | ~8 | "The root *mit* is most closely associated with:" | Straight recall: `notes/root-words.md` |
| **Word → its root's idea** | ~6 | "*Circumstance* contains an element suggesting…", "*synonym* contains a root associated with…" | Split the word: circum + stance, syn + **nym** |
| **Synonym / meaning** | ~14 | "Closest synonym of *calumny*", "A *jejune* argument is…", "To *rescind* a regulation is to…" | Recall the list meaning. Beware options that just *sound* related |
| **Antonym** | ~12 | "Antonym of *languid*", "*Guile* refers to cunning. Its antonym is:" | **Three options are often synonyms of the word.** The odd one out is the answer |
| **Root–meaning pairs** | ~1 | "Which pair is correctly matched / INCORRECT?" | Check each pair against the root table |

**Most-repeated questions**, appearing in all four sets: *morbid* (antonym healthy), *fact/fac* (manufacture), *knave* (dishonest person), *philanthropy* (love of humanity), *morph* (transformation of form). See `answer-keys/question-pool.md`, sorted by repeats.

## 2. Aptitude & reasoning MCQs (self-assessment format)

30 questions, (a)–(d). Here's the mix on the sheet and what to expect for each type:

| Type | Share | Expect |
|---|---|---|
| **Clocks** | 5/30 | Hands coinciding, opposite or at right angles; a clock gaining or losing time |
| **Coding-decoding** | 5/30 | +n shift, growing shift, reversal, letter → number, pair swaps |
| **Number series** | 5/30 | Differences (squares or constant second difference), ×n + c, two interleaved series |
| **Time & work** | 4/30 | M×H×D/W formula, men ↔ women ↔ boys conversion |
| **Letter series** | 3/30 | Each letter position moves by a fixed or growing step, alpha-numeric terms |
| **Ratio** | 2/30 | ak : bk with a change; chaining A:B and B:C |
| **Partnership** | 2/30 | Capital × months; "joined after m months" |
| Ages, provisions, games of skill, variation | 1 each | See `notes/aptitude.md` |

**Speed tips:** write A1 E5 J10 O15 T20 Y25 at the top of your rough sheet before starting. Clock answers almost always end in **/11**; if your answer doesn't, re-check the method. Skip long calculations on the first pass and come back to them.

## 3. Written questions (worksheet format)

If there's a written part, it will look like the worksheets:

- **Make a sentence** using an idiom or foreign phrase (Idioms Q1, Foreign Part B)
- **Write the idiom** for a given meaning (Idioms Q2)
- **Fill in the blank** with the right foreign expression from four options (Foreign Part A)

**Sentence formula that scores:** put the phrase in a context that *proves you know the meaning*.

> Weak: "He is a loose cannon."
> Strong: "The new striker is a loose cannon; nobody knows what he'll do next."

Get proper nouns right: *Ivy League* (the eight US universities), *Good Samaritan*, *Peeping Tom*. For Latin terms, italics aren't needed in handwriting, but spell them exactly: *habeas corpus*, *prima facie*, *curriculum vitae*.
