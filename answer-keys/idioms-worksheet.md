## Q1. Sentences (model answers — write your own in the exam)

| Idiom | Meaning | Sentence |
|---|---|---|
| **Head Over Heels** | Very excited and/or joyful, especially when in love. | Riya fell head over heels for the city the moment she moved there. |
| **Mumbo Jumbo** | Nonsense or meaningless speech. | The salesman's pitch was pure mumbo jumbo — nobody understood a word of it. |
| **Buy A Lemon** | To purchase a vehicle that constantly gives problems or stops running after you drive it away. | He bought a lemon — the second-hand bike broke down on the ride home. |
| **Let The Cat Out Of The Bag** | To share a secret that wasn't supposed to be shared. | My brother let the cat out of the bag about Mom's surprise party. |
| **Van Gogh's ear for music** | Tone deaf. | Don't ask me to judge the singing contest; I have Van Gogh's ear for music. |
| **No Dice** | To not agree. To not accept a proposition. | I asked the warden for a late pass, but it was no dice. |
| **Jaywalk** | Crossing the street (from the middle) without using the crosswalk. | He was fined for jaywalking across the highway instead of using the zebra crossing. |
| **Zero Tolerance** | No crime or law breaking big or small will be overlooked | Our college has zero tolerance for ragging. |
| **Pass The Buck** | Avoid responsibility by giving it to someone else. | When the project failed, the manager tried to pass the buck to his juniors. |
| **Knock On Wood** | Knuckle tapping on wood in order to avoid some bad luck. | I haven't missed a single deadline this semester — knock on wood. |
| **Excuse my French** | Please forgive me for cussing. | Excuse my French, but this traffic is bloody awful. |
| **A black sheep** | An odd or disreputable member of a group, especially within a family. | He was the black sheep of the family, the only one who never finished school. |
| **Good Samaritan** | Someone who helps others when they are in need, with no discussion for compensation, and no thought of a reward. | A Good Samaritan rushed the injured cyclist to hospital and left without giving his name. |
| **Know the Ropes** | To understand the details. | Ask Priya to train you; she knows the ropes in this department. |
| **Skid Row** | The rundown area of a city where the homeless and drug users live. | After losing his job and his savings, he ended up on skid row. |
| **Use Your Loaf** | Use your head. Think smart. | Use your loaf — carry an umbrella when the sky looks like that! |
| **Peeping Tom** | Someone who observes people in the nude or sexually active people, mainly for his own gratification. | The police arrested a Peeping Tom who was spying through hostel windows. |
| **Ivy League** | Since 1954 the Ivy League has been the following universities: Columbia, Brown, Cornell, Dartmouth, Yale, Pennsylvania, Princeton, and Harvard. | She won a scholarship to Princeton, an Ivy League university. |
| **Get Down to Brass Tacks** | To become seriously about something. | Enough small talk; let's get down to brass tacks and discuss the budget. |
| **New York Minute** | A minute that seems to go by quickly, especially in a fast paced environment. | The concert tickets sold out in a New York minute. |
| **Run out of steam** | To be completely out of energy. | I ran out of steam after studying for eight hours straight. |
| **Tongue-in-cheek** | Humor, not to be taken seriously. | His tongue-in-cheek review praised the film for being 'perfectly boring'. |
| **Scapegoat** | Someone else who takes the blame. | The intern was made the scapegoat for the manager's mistake. |
| **Queer the pitch** | Destroy or ruin a plan. | Leaking our plan to the rivals queered the pitch for the whole deal. |
| **Loose Cannon** | Someone who is unpredictable and can cause damage if not kept in check. | The new striker is a loose cannon; no one knows what he'll do next. |
| **High on the Hog** | Living in Luxury. | After the promotion, they started living high on the hog. |

_Note: the sheet prints “Jumbo Jumbo” — it means **Mumbo Jumbo**._
